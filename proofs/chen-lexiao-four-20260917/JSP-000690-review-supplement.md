The official entry asks whether a three-uniform, three-chromatic-critical hypergraph with minimum degree at least seven exists. [Erdős Problem 834](https://www.erdosproblems.com/834) cites `[Er74d, p.282]` and distinguishes two readings of “critical.” This submission follows the catalog's explicit weak vertex-coloring reading. It does not silently replace it by a hitting-set or transversal-number statement.

## Exact definitions and all quantifiers

Both sources use `Fin 9`, labeling the paper's vertices `1,…,9` as `0,…,8`. An edge is a finite set, and the edge family is a finite set of those sets, so repeated edges and repeated vertices cannot count multiple times. A's explicit list also carries a kernel-checked no-duplicate proof. Uniformity means every edge has exactly three vertices. Degree counts distinct incident edges.

`ProperColoring E c` says that for each edge there exist two vertices in it with different colors. This is weak coloring: an edge need not have three different colors. All functions to the selected palette are quantified over, not just a supplied list of purportedly representative colorings.

In A, `FiniteHypergraph` stores a vertex set, an edge family and a proof every edge lies in that vertex set. `IsProperSubgraph K H` requires inclusion of both sets and inequality of at least one. `IsThreeColorCritical H` combines exact chromatic number three with two-colorability of **every** such proper subgraph. `HasChromaticNumber E 3` explicitly rules out every smaller natural palette, including zero and one. The final `solution` at line 268 asserts an unconditional witness, nine vertices, 22 edges, uniformity, criticality, all degrees at least seven and an attained degree seven.

Coloring functions in A are written on the ambient `Fin 9`; for a subgraph they restrict to its actual retained vertex subtype because every edge lies there. Conversely, a coloring on that subtype extends arbitrarily on omitted vertices for the positive palettes used here. Empty subgraphs cause no problem: there are no edge obligations and the two-color palette is inhabited. We do not claim the corresponding restriction-equivalence lemma itself has been added to this frozen source.

In B, `CriticallyThreeChromatic` explicitly contains three-colorability, failure of two-colorability, all single-edge deletion colorings and all single-vertex deletion colorings. Its final `solution` at line 118 also establishes uniformity and attained minimum degree seven. The fixed type has nine ambient vertices; the particular witness has the same 22 edges, but B's final theorem does not separately assert `H.card=22`. B does not contain A's named arbitrary-proper-subgraph theorem or generic exact-chromatic-number predicate. These are interface differences, not omitted cases of the original chromatic existence answer: every smaller palette embeds into a two-color palette, and the deletion families imply the standard proper-subgraph condition by the argument below.

Vertex deletion discards all incident edges instead of shrinking them. Neither version assumes a transversal bound, planarity, connectivity, regularity, or an unproved certificate property. The witness is not regular: its degree sequence is `(10,7,7,7,7,7,7,7,7)`. The theorem does not optimize the number of vertices or edges.

## Complete finite proof and structural reduction

The fixed `edges`/`witness` definitions supply all 22 triples. The following argument uses the finite data actually present in the sources; it does not assume that an external program reported success.

1. Count the set of triples and the three distinct vertices of each. Count the triples containing each of the nine labels; the resulting degrees are ten at zero and seven at every other label. This proves uniformity, positive degrees and an attained minimum of seven.
2. For any proposed binary coloring `c`, read its nine values. These form one of the `2^9=512` binary vectors. A proves `binary_vectors_fail` for all nine `Fin 2` arguments by kernel reduction, and `coloring_as_vector` proves that their vector is the original function. Thus no function is missed. B directly kernel-reduces the finite function quantifier in `witness_not_two_colorable`.
3. The vector `(0,0,1,0,0,1,2,1,1)` is a proper three-coloring. Along with step 2 it gives exact chromatic number three: any coloring with fewer than two colors embeds into a two-color palette. A records this monotonicity and the exact conclusion explicitly.
4. For each of the 22 possible deleted edges, the displayed binary vector colors all remaining edges properly. A's `edgeDeletionColoring_proper` checks the entire table; B checks its 22 individual cases. Each listed deleted edge is indeed the only monochromatic edge under its certificate, also confirming the intended deletion semantics.
5. If `F` is a strict edge subfamily, choose an original edge `e` absent from `F`. The certificate for deleting `e` is a proper coloring of `F`, since removing further edges cannot create a monochromatic edge. This is A's `every_strict_edge_subfamily_two_colorable`.
6. If a proper subhypergraph omits a vertex, its edge family cannot equal the original one: the omitted vertex has positive degree and an incident edge would not fit inside the retained vertex set. Thus every proper subhypergraph has a strict edge subfamily, and step 5 applies. A formalizes this in `proper_subgraph_has_fewer_edges` and `every_proper_subgraph_two_colorable`.
7. B separately supplies nine vertex-deletion certificates. To get the usual arbitrary-subgraph consequence from B's interface, either an edge is omitted and step 5 applies, or a vertex is omitted and the subgraph is contained in that single-vertex deletion. Restrict the resulting two-coloring to its actual vertex domain.

A further proves that each individual edge or vertex deletion retains at least one edge, so its chromatic number is **exactly** two, not merely at most two. Arbitrary proper subgraphs may be edgeless; for them only two-colorability is required. `solution` combines the already proved components and has no hypotheses.

An auxiliary Python inspection on 2026-09-26 extracted the data from both fixed source files, checked equality of their edge lists with plby's, enumerated all 512 binary functions, and checked the three-coloring, 44 edge-deletion certificates and nine B vertex-deletion certificates. It confirmed all the finite claims above. This is an additional transparent data cross-check, not a new Lean build, a proof of authorship, or an independent Lean kernel. The permanent verification report separately records actual Lean evidence.
