## Mathematical solution and exact conventions

**Complete mathematical solution and sources.** The positive answer is due to Noga Alon and Paul Erdős, *An Application of Graph Theory to Additive Number Theory*, European Journal of Combinatorics 6 (1985), 201–203, Theorem 1 and equation (4) on p.201, with proof on p.202. Their paper uses a distinct-summand convention; the present submission explicitly uses ordered convolution, including diagonal pairs, and a Sidon conclusion that also excludes diagonal collisions. The following complete finite argument records the adaptation used by the selected Lean implementation. The coefficient `4 + 24k` below is the explicit bound established by this implementation; it is not asserted to be the original paper's coefficient or a new best mathematical constant. Mathematical credit remains with the cited authors. The mathematical solution, convention correspondence and proposed formal statement are submitted for maintainer review; no prior maintainer approval or independent human review is claimed.

**Counting conventions.** Write `R_A(t) = #{(a,b) in A × A : a+b=t}` for the ordered count, `r_<(t) = #{(a,b) in A × A : a<b, a+b=t}`, and `d_A(t) = #{a in A : 2a=t}`. Since `A` is a set of natural numbers, `d_A(t)` is either zero or one, and `R_A(t)=2r_<(t)+d_A(t)`. Thus a bound `r_<(t)≤K` for distinct unordered pairs gives `R_A(t)≤2K+1`. If `r_≤` includes the diagonal, then `R_A=2r_≤−d_A`, so `r_≤≤K` gives `R_A≤2K`. The Lean statement assumes `R_A≤k` directly. Its Sidon condition says that every equality `a+b=c+d`, including repeated summands, has the same unordered pair on both sides. This implies the distinct-summand Sidon condition; the converse is false (for example, `{0,1,2}` has the nontrivial diagonal collision `0+2=1+1`). No equality of the two conventions at unchanged `k` is asserted.

**Complete finite proof.** Fix finite `A⊆ℕ`, let `n=|A|`, and suppose `R_A(t)≤k` for every `t`. Form the family `F3` of three-element sets `{a,b,c}⊆A` with `b+c=2a` and `b≠c`, and the family `F4` of four-element sets supporting an equality `a+b=c+d` with all four elements distinct. For each centre `a`, at most `k` ordered pairs represent `2a`; hence `|F3|≤kn`. For each ordered pair `(a,b)`, at most `k` ordered pairs represent `a+b`; hence `|F4|≤kn²`. Passing from pairs to forbidden sets can only decrease these counts. Each nontrivial equal-sum collision either contains three distinct elements and belongs to `F3`, or contains four distinct elements and belongs to `F4`: a common element across the two pairs cancels, and two diagonal pairs cancel in the natural numbers. Consequently a subset avoiding both families is Sidon under the stated strong convention.

For any `0≤p≤1`, select each element of `A` independently with probability `p`. Expected sample size is `pn`, and expected numbers of surviving forbidden sets are `|F3|p³` and `|F4|p⁴`. Some sample has size minus its number of surviving forbidden sets at least the expected difference. Delete at most one element from every surviving forbidden set. The resulting Sidon subset has size at least `pn−knp³−kn²p⁴`. This averaging statement is proved in Lean by finite nonnegative weights on the powerset, not taken as an extra probabilistic assumption.

Let `m` be the size of a largest Sidon subset of `A`, which exists because the powerset is finite and contains the empty set. If `A` is empty, the claimed inequality is immediate. Otherwise `m≥1`, and for every `p∈[0,1]`, `pn−knp³−kn²p⁴≤m`. If `n≤2m`, then `n²≤4m²≤4m³`. If `n>2m`, substitute `p=2m/n`; this gives `mn²≤8km³+16km⁴≤24km⁴`, and division by `m>0` gives `n²≤24km³`. Both cases imply

`n² ≤ (4+24k)m³`.

Thus `A` has a Sidon subset of size at least `c_k n^(2/3)`, where `c_k=(4+24k)^(-1/3)>0`. Since `n^(2/3)/sqrt(n)=n^(1/6)` tends to infinity, this exceeds every fixed multiple of `sqrt(n)` for all sufficiently large `n`. Since `2/3 > 7/12 = 1/2+1/12`, it also exceeds `n^(1/2+1/12)` for all sufficiently large `n`. The bounds are uniform over all admissible sets of each size. These are precisely the two eventual guarantees requested by the original problem. The targets are `JSP000633.finite_bound`, `JSP000633.sidon_subset_two_thirds`, `JSP000633.answer_sqrt`, and `JSP000633.answer_positive_power`.

**Extremal-function interface.** The code states the original conclusions through universal thresholds over finite sets instead of introducing an `H_k(n)` definition. If `H_k(n)` is defined as the largest guaranteed integer size capped by `n`, these thresholds give the corresponding limit and positive-power conclusions. The cap resolves vacuous cases: for ordered counts, `k=1` permits no set of size at least two, and then the capped universal guarantee is `H_1(n)=n`. The source is stronger in allowing `k=0`; the empty case is handled, and nonempty admissible sets then do not exist. The requested original range `k≥1` is included.

## Comparison with complete prior formalizations

[PR #454](https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/pull/454) already submits a complete formalization of both questions. Its pinned [Definitions.lean](https://github.com/CollinYuanjieRen/awards/blob/32403814a463c9c1643c81409a0f76f5707bcbcd/submissions/jsp-000633-cyr/Erdos772Sidon/Definitions.lean) uses exactly the same ordered representation count and strong Sidon condition as this submission. Its [Bounds.lean](https://github.com/CollinYuanjieRen/awards/blob/32403814a463c9c1643c81409a0f76f5707bcbcd/submissions/jsp-000633-cyr/Erdos772Sidon/Bounds.lean), theorem `Erdos772.H_lower`, gives an integer `s` satisfying `n²<6k(s+1)³` and `s≤2H_k(n)`. For `k≥1`, its displayed consequence is therefore

`H_k(n) > (48k)^(-1/3)n^(2/3) − 1/2`.

The submitted bound is `m≥(4+24k)^(-1/3)n^(2/3)` for each admissible set. Because `4+24k<48k` for `k≥1`, it improves the real-valued lower-bound expression just displayed, with no subtractive one-half term. The implementation also differs: #454 averages over subsets of fixed cardinality and counts ordered bad tuples; this submission averages using finite Bernoulli weights, counts three- and four-element forbidden sets separately, and optimizes the resulting inequality at `p=2m/n`. This is a concrete comparison of named bounds and implementation components, not a claim that every integer-rounded consequence of #454 is strictly weaker, that the mathematical constant is optimal, or that this is the first complete formalization. Both submissions acknowledge the earlier complete plby formalization, which remains an additional comparator.


The other explicitly compared source is [plby's fixed Erdos772.lean](https://github.com/plby/lean-proofs/blob/8822f7ddef30fadbd92e1c6ab4ed897af356af5e/src/latest/ErdosProblems/Erdos772.lean), registered in [issue #19](https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/issues/19). Its `exists_sidon_finset_cubic` assumes the same ordered product/filter count, includes diagonal pairs and proves `card(A)^2 ≤ 4096*(k+1)^3*card(S)^3` with the same strong Sidon property. That source already proves both original asymptotic questions. The coefficient `4+24k` improves the specified cubic coefficient. This comparison neither attributes that earlier implementation to this submitter nor establishes first-formalization priority or the optimal mathematical constant.
